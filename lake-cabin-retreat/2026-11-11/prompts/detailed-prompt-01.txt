── THE FLIGHT · 7 legs, architecture A (one continuous forward take, no connectors) ──
 1. arrival across the lake, rising onto the deck
 2. the great room      3. the kitchen        4. the reading nook
 3. the bedroom         6. the soaking bath   7. back out over the water

── THE SEAM LAW · this is what makes it one take instead of seven clips ──
· Leg 1 starts from the hero render.
· Every later leg's `--start-image` is the PREVIOUS leg's ACTUAL last frame, pulled with ffmpeg:
    ffmpeg -sseof -0.15 -i leg{n}.mp4 -frames:v 1 last.png
  Never a source still, never a job id. This is the only thing that makes seams frame-identical.
· Give every leg an `--end-image` of the real room render it must arrive at. The skill says architecture A takes no end-image — override that here. Prompt-only fidelity drifts badly: unpinned, the model invents a different building with different furniture. These end-images are interiors ahead of the camera, so motion stays forward and the pull-back stutter that rule guards against never happens. Leave leg 7 unpinned only if you want it to fly out; pin it to the hero render if you want it to land there.
· Render the legs SEQUENTIALLY — each one needs the previous one to exist. Look at every leg's last frame before starting the next. A bad handoff frame poisons every leg after it.
· If you later need to redo one leg, pin its `--end-image` to the NEXT leg's existing first frame. That preserves the rest of the chain instead of forcing a full re-render.

── WRITE EVERY LEG PROMPT WITH ──
· The room's real materials, read off the render — not invented. Name the sofa colour, the stove, the counter, the fixtures.
· "The camera only ever moves forward and never pulls back", plus a closing clause: "in the final second, settle into a slow steady forward glide toward [the next room]".
· A fixed grade, or the legs drift orange: "cool blue-teal dusk ambient, warm amber only from lamps and fire, natural white point, do not oversaturate into orange".
· "Empty, unoccupied, no people, architectural, tasteful" — Seedance's content filter flags perfectly innocuous interiors. Budget 2–3 re-rolls per interior leg; it usually passes on the second try. If one keeps failing, re-render just that leg on `kling3_0` with the same start/end frames.

── FIRST AND LAST FRAME ──
Composite the hero render onto the head of leg 1 and the tail of the final leg with a ~0.35s alpha fade, so the flight opens and closes on exactly that image. Pure ffmpeg, costs nothing, and removes any drift the model introduced at the ends.

── ENCODE ──
Native 1080p, `-crf 20 -g 8 -movflags +faststart -an`, and save each clip's first frame as a poster.

── EMBEDDING THE ENGINE · it is built to own an entire page, so wire all of this or it will break the site ──
· Every engine layer is `position:fixed;inset:0` and never releases. Scope them and release once the hero has scrolled past, otherwise the opaque backdrop sits over the whole rest of the page and nothing below the hero is reachable.
· Do NOT set `opacity` on the engine's container to fade it — an opacity below 1 makes it the containing block for its own fixed children and the pinning collapses. Fade the layers individually.
· The engine wraps its CSS in `@layer`. An unlayered host reset like `*{margin:0;padding:0}` beats layered CSS at any specificity and will flatten every padding and margin in the flight. Inject the engine CSS unlayered, and delete its `html,body` rule so it stops restyling the host page.
· Change any wrapper's `overflow-x:hidden` to `overflow-x:clip`. `hidden` forces `overflow-y` to compute to `auto`, which spawns a second scrollbar over the whole page.
· Hide the route rail's text labels; keep the dots.
· A scene's poster is its FIRST frame. If you hold the last scene on its final frame, paint the intended still explicitly — otherwise the wrong frame flashes whenever that clip has not finished loading.

── KEEP THE ORIGINAL HERO ──
Do not delete the existing hero content. Keep the wordmark, the headline, the star rating and the booking card, pinned over the first frame, and fade them out over the first ~0.7 screens as the camera moves in. Do not invent replacement copy.

── HOW IT ENDS ──
When the flight finishes, hold the final frame and fade that same hero overlay back IN, so dates can still be picked. Then cross-dissolve into the next section as it rises behind. Releasing straight from the flight into the next section shows a full screen of black — the section's last screenful is empty. Use a smoothstep on the dissolve so it passes through the half-and-half blend quickly.

── QA BEFORE HANDING IT BACK ──
Drive it in a real browser with playwright — NOT an embedded preview pane. Preview panes report the page as hidden, which throttles requestAnimationFrame to zero and makes the engine look completely broken when it is fine. Verify: scroll drives currentTime, every seam holds, the hero fades out and back in, the tail holds, the release dissolves with no black frame, the footer is reachable, and the console is clean."
